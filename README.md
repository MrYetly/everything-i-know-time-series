Around 2021, I listened to a podcast with the economist Tyler Cowen in which he said, "what is it you do every day that is comparable to a pianist practicing scales?" I thought about this question for a long time, unable to find an answer. Now, I think I have found one, and it is this project.

This project started because I have worked on time series problems sporadically throughout my education and career. The gap between these efforts has been big enough that I often find myself needing to brush up on fundamentals before I apply myself. This is quite annoying and boring, because I often find myself skimming over pages that are just familiar enough to make me feel like I understand them already, and yet just foreign enough that I invevitably get to the topic I really want to understand only to find that some prerequisite concept is still eluding me. In my first semester econometrics class of my PhD, Dr. Carruther's, the professor, told us about how she made her own textbooks to study for her comprehesive exams, titled "Everything I know about X", where X was microeconomics, macroeconomics, etc. That struck me as a good idea then, and now it strikes me as a good way to keep my foundations solid and my ability to apply myself fluent. The goal of this currently nascent work is to force myself to maintain my own body of knowledge: if I learn something new, I add it; if I find that there is a gap in my knowledge as I try to learn something new, I go back into my foundation and fill it. Critically, I try to make all of my tree of knowledge cohesive, both up and down from where I insert something new. This building of cohesion has always been one of the most satisfying parts of learning, and so it is a very nice carrot to place ahead of myself.

This project has started with time series, but I plan on doing this for all of my technical expertise: causal inference, experimentation, and perhaps eventually machine learning and economic theory. There's only so much time in a day, a week, a month, a career.
